- Concurrent writers can no longer fork the archive with two records at the same sequence. A hook process that had cached its view of the archive could, when the head hint did not validate (a writer interrupted between sealing its record and updating the hint), read the chain tail from that stale view and seal a second record at a sequence already on disk - after which every later write refused with "chain broken". The tail is now always re-read from disk under the write lock, every sequence is claimed exclusively before its record is written (a second writer of the same sequence re-reads the tail and retries instead of forking), and the fallback lock used where kernel locking is unavailable is taken over only from a holder that is provably dead, never from one that is merely slow.
