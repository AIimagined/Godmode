# Godmode v0.3.3

The advisory finds its audience.

v0.3.2 shipped the loops; the same night, four hosts ran them live and
seventeen field reports came back. Eleven fragments fold in here, and
every one answers a sentence someone actually wrote from a real session.

## The echoes: what the boundaries now carry to the model

Three reports in a row measured the same shape from different angles: the
right message was being delivered to the wrong audience, or at the wrong
time. The Stop hook's unrecorded-claim advisory rendered to the operator -
the model that made the claim never saw it, so fifteen sessions tallied
the claim verb unused while the deterrent pointed away from its target.
The continuity brief printed on SessionStart stdout - which Grok ignores,
so resume stayed a manual step on that host. And an obligation recorded
mid-session surfaced at resume and session close - inert, as one report
put it, for the entire useful middle.

All three now ride the same parking contract: bounded content parked
beside the archive (never in it), delivered exactly once at the next
prompt boundary, then deleted. The claim advisory reaches the model with
the flagged sentences and the recording command. The brief reaches hosts
that ignore SessionStart. An open obligation whose vocabulary the turn's
reply touches is named at the Stop boundary and handed to the model next
turn. The first live use of `claim --grade hypothesis` elsewhere showed
what the nudge buys: a graded hypothesis that was later refuted cost
nothing to retract.

One honesty line on the Grok brief: the parking and one-shot delivery are
pinned in tests, and Grok documents UserPromptSubmit as observe-only -
whether its live runtime surfaces the delivered context to the model
awaits a fresh session's chronicle. Until that lands, resume on Grok
remains a skill step, and this document does not claim otherwise.

## The law loop, hardened by its own first week

A standing instruction lands as a candidate on first telling and promotes
after one session - and the first live promotion promptly found three
gaps, all closed: a promoted cluster is now consumed (it stayed
"promotable" forever and invited a duplicate law), the compiler keeps one
law per subject with the newest record winning (which also makes retiring
by appended record real), and candidate keywords drop trailing
punctuation while a passage the operator said to disregard feeds neither
detector. Separately, committing the generated law file had minted seven
unattested HARD charter rules from its own guard sentences within the
hour - a record of guards is a record, so the code-of-law role now
compiles ADVISORY at most, the same cap the lessons ledger already has.

## Each host, again by its own contract

- **Grok**: detection keys on the variables its hook subprocess actually
  injects (`GROK_PLUGIN_ROOT`/`GROK_HOOK_EVENT`) and on its read-only
  builtin names at the shape step - the builtin the gate used to deny ran
  live, `hooks status` read HARD, and the host table's claim now stands
  on that chronicle rather than the lab pin that first carried it. The
  declared-events list adds Stop and PostToolUse.
- **OpenCode**: a deny relayed through the shim records an ACKNOWLEDGED
  interception proof - the shim's documented throw stops the tool - so
  one live blocked call upgrades the grade the host table promises, while
  a self-injected probe still cannot.
- **Every removal-shaped ask or deny** (filesystem mutation, worktree
  discard, local repository change) names the godmode-governance preview
  in its reason: twice in one day the designed net sat uninvoked while
  reading discipline caught the mistake, so the boundary now carries the
  skill's name to the moment.
